# Session 5 — The Coronation

> **Second-to-last session.** Picks up where the table actually left off: Lux Æterna evacuated under Legio Null pressure, the party rode east with the Band (and, unknowingly, the Setting hidden in Torvin's compass), drew the pursuit off, and made camp within sight of the **Ruin of Sorath** — the First Age structure on the hill, not the Mining Camp the party already cleared weeks ago. This is a new site: Torvin's crew spotted it during their original expedition, marked it, and never went in. **Tajsh stayed behind** at Lux Æterna to save his archive during the evacuation — he is not with the party tonight, and that changes how the book gets read. Tonight: a short dive into the ruin itself, the guardian fight, the pedestal, the book — ripped open to reveal the Gem hidden in its spine — and the Architect's return. He assembles the Crown himself, from a distance, without touching a thing. He puts it on. **He is not a god. He is the greatest thing a god ever made, and he has decided the throne should be his.** He takes a shape it hasn't worn in fifteen centuries, staggers under the weight of being real again — and when the party pushes, he laughs, tells them exactly where he's going, and brings the ruin down as he goes.
>
> **Next session (the finale): the team-up.** The party — now allied with Commander Thalric and whatever's left of his column — has to figure out how to pull a crown off a half-formed usurper before he finishes recovering, in the one place he's always been strongest: the ground under Lux Æterna. Sabel and Tajsh are there. So is everyone the town couldn't evacuate in time.

**Cast (PCs):** Vorn (dwarf), Valeth (Aen'valar elf), Yldroth (elf monk), Norseman (human battle-mage), Arden (Drakona mystic — *hidden*)
**Key NPCs:** The Architect (embodied, revealed), Commander Thalric (arrives at the very end)
**Absent, alive, load-bearing for next session:** Tajsh Thale (saving his archive at Lux Æterna), Warden Sabel (holding what's left of the evacuation), Old Cira (Lux Æterna)
**Target:** ~3.5–4 hrs

### GM-only: the identity reveal, sourced
This is the load-bearing lore beat of the session. Labeling per the ground rules — (a) established fact, (b) inference bridging established facts, (c) newly invented specifics — so you can veto or adjust freely before running it.

**The Architect is not a god. It is Kael'Zorai's creation — the greatest of them, and the one nobody remembers exists.**

- **(a)** Kael'Zorai is already canon as **"the Fifth First Hand"** — created independently, codified Magic and Time, created the Circles Below as "a separate dimension for unconstrained experimentation," and was "stripped of name and authority" and sealed at the Binding after the Paroxysm. *(People/Kael'Zorai.md)*
- **(a)** The Circles Below, once the Paroxysm hit it, spawned three kinds of beings: Aberrations, Riftborn, and — this is the load-bearing one — **"Echo-entities."** *(World/The Circles Below.md)*
- **(a)** The Architect's own species field literally reads **"Echo-Lord."** Its file already states outright: *"It is not a god. It is not a servant of the Four."* *(Campaign/People/The Architect.md)*
- **(a)** The dwarves already call Kael'Zorai, specifically, **"Ulzammar — the Sealed Concept."** The Ulzammar (the vanished people) tried a harmonic working to reach the withdrawn Four, and it caused the Paroxysm instead. *(People/Kael'Zorai.md; Species/The Ulzammar.md)*
- **(b) — the bridge, no invention required:** the Architect is the greatest Echo-entity the Circles Below ever produced — Kael'Zorai's own creation, made in that realm before or during the Paroxysm. When the Ulzammar's ritual reached for the withdrawn Four and found the Fifth instead, mid-Binding, it wasn't Kael'Zorai himself who got caught in the Crown — it was this: his instrument, his echo, close enough to his will to carry a piece of the plan forward. The dwarven name "Ulzammar" for Kael'Zorai and the vanished people's own name were never a coincidence — dwarven lore has been unknowingly correct for fifteen centuries about what's sealed in that Crown, even if it never named the Architect specifically.
- **(c) — new tonight:** the Architect has spent 1,500 years as a voice with no body, "imprisoned at the Paroxysm," gathering the pieces of an instrument that was never really about escaping — it was about becoming real enough to finish what its maker started, with or without him. It is not the whole god. It never was. It's the servant who outlasted the master, alone long enough to decide the throne should be his. **Sauron, not Morgoth.** This also makes the **cold vulnerability structural, not asserted:** the Architect was born in the same broken dimension that produces every Scar-spawn the party has fought all campaign. Same origin, same weakness. That's the point of §7's beat below.

**Bookkeeping (do after the session, not before):** once played, this reveal should get written back into `People/The Architect.md` and cross-linked from `People/Kael'Zorai.md` — add to the existing bookkeeping list in `ENDGAME-PLAN.md` §7 rather than editing now.

## §1 — Camp & Approach (10–15 min)

> *Dawn over the foothills. Not the low scrub around the Mining Camp — a different hill, taller, the kind that makes its own shadow before the sun's fully up. Torvin's journal marked it once, in passing, with three words in a hand that wasn't his: "Not this one." Nobody in his crew ever explained what that meant. Nobody asked twice.*

Let this be quiet. A few prompts, use what fits:
- **Whoever carries the Band** feels it lean toward the hill before anyone's said a word about direction.
- **Whoever carries Cira's dream-catcher**: the threads have been restless since last night. This morning they're doing something new — pulling taut, then slack, then taut, in a rhythm that isn't wind.
- **Perception ◆◆ (optional)**: the air over the hill hums, one low note. Different from the pit at the Mining Camp — this one has a shape to it, almost architectural, like the note is coming from something built on purpose.

No mechanical stakes here — this is the held breath before the delve. Move to §2 whenever the table's ready.

## §2 — The Ruin of Sorath: A Short Dive (~30 min) — PROTECT

> *This is not the Mining Camp. There's no collapsed tents, no fused glass slag, nothing the party's already picked over. This hill has a doorway in it — half-buried, First Age stonework, the kind Torvin's crew clearly found and clearly chose not to open. Tonight it opens for someone.*

Keep this brisk and physical — three short beats, classic dungeon shape, in and out in about half an hour. This is not the session's danger budget; save that for the Unveiling and the Collapse.

### A — The Threshold (~5 min)
> *The door is stone, man-height, carved with the same three-ring mark that's followed you since the mine — except here it isn't scattered. It's a single mark, enormous, centered, deliberate. Beneath it, scratched crudely into the stone in a hand that isn't First Age at all: two words, recent, maybe a year old. **"NOT THIS."***

**Perception ◆◆:** the scratch marks were made in a hurry, by someone leaving fast, not arriving cautious. Whoever carved this wasn't warning the next crew about a monster. They were warning them about themselves.

The door isn't locked, isn't trapped, isn't guarded. It just opens — too easily, for something everyone who's seen it has chosen to leave shut.

### B — The Trap Corridor (~5–10 min)
> *A straight passage down, walls lined rank on rank with the three-ring mark — hundreds of them, marching into the dark like a warning repeated until the carver's hand gave out. And under your feet, faint, felt more than heard: a pulse. Steady. Patient. Almost a heartbeat, if a heartbeat could be measured with a ruler.*

**The trap:** the floor tiles are keyed to that pulse — this corridor was built to test whether whatever walked it belonged to order or not. **Perception ◆◆** notices the rhythm in time to use it; without that check, treat the first crossing attempt as blind.

- **Match the pulse** (Coordination or Cool ◆◆, once per PC, or one check to walk the party through together at Discipline ◆◆◆): cross clean.
- **Fail, or don't bother trying:** the tile underfoot drops a half-inch and the walls answer — a volley of grinding stone-teeth snapping from the carved ranks at ankle height. **Athletics ◆◆** to avoid; failure is 3 damage and a very loud, very unmissable alarm-note that rings the length of the corridor.

*(Nice color beat, not a puzzle to belabor: if a player tries something clever — humming the pulse back at the walls, having Vorn read the stonework, using Valeth's anchor-note to "answer" the rhythm rather than just matching it — reward it. This is the ruin's one moment of asking the party to be orderly before it asks them, upstairs, to be anything but.)*

If the alarm-note rings, skip ahead: the guardians in §2C are already moving before the party reaches them.

### C — The Guardian Hall (~15–20 min)
> *The corridor opens into a hall broken pillars, a First Age ceiling half fallen in, cold pooling along the floor like ground-fog from somewhere further down and further wrong. And rising out of the drifted sand between the pillars: shapes you've fought before — but many, and moving in step, in rhythm, in order.*

The synchronized guardian fight — this is the table's cold-and-song combo getting one more workout before it matters against something much bigger. No Tajsh in a doorway shouting useful things this time; the party reads the room on their own.

```
SCORCH-WRAITHS — run as 2 groups of 2 (Rival stats, grouped for speed)
Brawn 2 | Agility 3 | Intellect 1 | Cunning 2 | Willpower 2 | Presence 1
Wounds: 8 each | Soak: 3 | Defense: 0/0
Skills: Brawl 2, Perception 1
  Glass claws — Brawl · Damage 6 · Crit 3 · Engaged · Burn 1
ABILITIES
• Immune to Fire. | • Vulnerable to Cold (+2 damage; disables Geometric
  Eyes 1 round). | • Blindsight. | • Shatter (defeated: Coordination ◆ at
  Engaged or 3 piercing).
• Geometric Eyes (maneuver) — heat beam, Short line: Coordination ◆◆ or
  8 fire damage.
• IN-STEP (tonight only) — while the wraiths move in unison they add ◼
  to all checks. ANY dissonance breaks it for a full round: Valeth's
  anchor-note, clashing metal dragged on stone, deliberately arrhythmic
  attacks (player describes fighting UGLY), a cold effect. Broken rhythm:
  they lose the ◼ and grant ◼ to attacks against them.
```

**SONG-AND-FROST** (Valeth's signature): sings into his anchor-stone, directs the cold pooling along the floor at a group (Action — Knowledge (Arcana) or Discipline ◆◆; no anchor charge spent unless he also wants a ward):
- **Success:** 4 cold damage (ignores Soak), disables Geometric Eyes 1 round, breaks IN-STEP for a full round.
- **Triumph:** the group is immobilized 1 round.
- **Despair:** 2 strain to Valeth — the hum under the floor climbs a step.

Any PC can improvise cold — treat as a cold effect, +2 damage per vulnerability. **Creative cold always does something.**

**Teaching beat:** first PC to invoke a Sabel lesson aloud ("fight ugly," "they like order," "sing it cold") gives the party ◼ next round and +5 XP at wrap.

Win = clear both groups or drop them to a single wraith, who shatters and flees deeper into the hall, toward a door at the far end — sealed once, broken recently, by picks. Torvin's crew got this far. Not one step further. → §3.

## §3 — The Pedestal & the Book (10–15 min) — MUST HIT

> *The chamber past the broken seal is small. Round. Unadorned — after a hall of ten thousand warnings, this room says nothing at all, which is worse.*
>
> *In the center: a pedestal of pale metal, the same veins as the Band. On it, exactly as Torvin wrote it: a book. Bound in metal, its pages some thin crystal leaf, thicker than a brick, undisturbed by fifteen hundred years of dust — because the dust will not land on it.*
>
> *No gem. No recess. Just the book, humming Valeth's note, and — quietly, from someone's coat pocket — a second hum answering it. If nobody checks their pockets, nobody notices. Yet.*

*(That second hum is the Foreman's compass — the Setting. Say it once, mildly, buried mid-paragraph. Players who catch it, catch it. If the tell already fired at your table during the ride east, skip this line — the compass is already known.)*

**Lifting the book** is easy — it doesn't resist, doesn't warn, doesn't want anything from whoever touches it. That should feel wrong before anyone can say why.

**Opening it** is not easy. The binding has no visible clasp. **Perception ◆◆** finds a hairline seam running the length of the spine — old, deliberate, meant to be found by someone who knew to look, not forced by someone who didn't. Without that check, the only way in is force: **Brawn/Athletics ◆◆◆**, and it costs — the crystal-leaf pages don't shatter clean, they scream, a thin, awful, almost-musical note that every PC in the room feels in their teeth (1 strain, party-wide).

**Either way, the spine splits — and that's when the light in the room changes.** → §4. Do not let the party examine what's inside before the Architect speaks; he's faster than they are.

## §4 — The Architect Returns (20–30 min) — MUST HIT

> *The light doesn't come from anywhere. It arrives — the geometry blooming from nothing, planes and rings, the way it did in the Deep Chamber, the way it did at the Completion you never quite reached — and the voice, warm, delighted, a little breathless:*
>
> *"You found it faster than I dared hope."*

**Play this warm, but hurried.** He's not the patient mentor from the first manifestation — there's an edge under the warmth tonight, something close to hunger held on a short leash. He doesn't ask how they're doing. He doesn't take questions the way he used to. He has a schedule now.

> *"I can read this. Give me a moment none of you have — you won't feel it pass."*

**The translation (deliver it as performance, not exposition — he's reading aloud, and enjoying it):**
> *"'Three made one made silence.' It's a record. An account of a binding — the last working the Ulzammar ever performed, and the one that unmade them. They thought they were building an instrument to call something home. They were building a cage. They didn't know the difference. Very few ever do."*

*(This is true, and it is also the Architect describing itself without saying so. A Perception ◆◆◆ or a player who's been paying attention all campaign can feel the wrongness of how personally he's reading it — like a man narrating his own obituary and smiling.)*

> *"And here — of course. Of course he'd hide it here." A pause, almost fond. "The Gem was never lost. It was never on this pedestal at all. It's been inside the book the entire time — bound into the spine, where anyone clever enough to open it would have to want it badly enough to break something beautiful to get it. That was always the test."*

**The Gem:** nested in the shattered spine, dark, faceted, fist-small. It lifts free without resistance. **Discipline ◆◆** on whoever takes it — failure: 2 strain and an involuntary image (a crown, whole, on a brow of light, and every living thing standing perfectly, permanently still). Success: same image, and the certainty it was *sent.*

> *"Now. Set it down. All of it — the Band, the Gem. You've carried them far enough."*

→ §5.

## §5 — The Completion (15–20 min) — CLIFFHANGER build

### The instruction
> *"The book tells you how to set them," the Architect says, and for a moment it sounds like every other time it's coached you through something — patient, precise, almost kind. "The Band at the base. The Setting around it, seam to seam — you'll feel where. The Gem last, and only once the first two have gone still. Whoever's hands are steadiest should—"*

**Let a player start to move.** Let someone actually reach for the Band, expecting to do this the way they've done everything else tonight: together, with their own hands, coached from a comfortable distance.

> *It stops them with nothing but a change in tone.*
>
> *"No. Not you. I was speaking from habit — the habit of fifteen hundred years of borrowing hands because I had none of my own. I find, tonight, that I no longer need to. Stand back. Don't touch them. I won't either — not with anything you'd recognize as a hand."*

### The assembly, at range
> *The Band lifts itself out of whoever's pack is carrying it — not violently, not even quickly, just the way a leaf lifts when the wind finally gets under it — and settles into the air above the broken book. The Gem follows. Neither of them asks permission from the hands that were holding them a moment ago.*
>
> *"I did tell you once that I cannot move stones," the Architect says, and there is something almost like an apology in it. "That was true when I said it. I am not asking these pieces to move. I am reminding them what they are for. There is a difference, and you are about to watch me not care whether you understand it."*

**If a PC tries to grab a piece out of the air, or attack the Architect directly here:** he is not fully embodied yet — attacks pass through the geometry like smoke, and a grab for a floating piece requires **Athletics ◆◆◆◆**; success snatches it free for one round before the pull drags it back (contested Brawn/Athletics each round it's held, escalating). This buys the table a genuine near-miss and a good story beat, not a way to stop what's coming — the Crown completes this session regardless. Let the attempt matter anyway: reward it with **+10 XP** at wrap and let that PC own the "we tried" moment the rest of the table remembers.

### The third hum
> *And then the third hum — the quiet one, the pocket one — gets louder. Torvin's brass compass is glowing along its seams in whoever's pack is carrying it. The needle spins free. The engraving — "Come home" — fills with light, letter by letter, like something reading it aloud to itself.*
>
> *"And there she is," the Architect says, softer than anything he's said tonight. "Your foreman was cleverer than his gods. I confess I lost track of that one for a while."*
>
> *The brass sloughs away like wax. Underneath: a ring of pale First Age metal — a setting, made to hold a gem, made to bind a band. It rises to join the other two, unresisting, unhurried, home.*

*(Skip this beat entirely — cut straight to the Completion below — if the compass tell already fired during the ride east at your table.)*

### The Completion — read slowly
> *The three pieces meet in the air above the broken book, and there is one clear, triumphant note — and for exactly one heartbeat it is the most beautiful sound any of you will ever hear.*
>
> *Then it keeps going. Past beauty. Past music. Into something that isn't sound anymore so much as the absence everything else has been standing on top of.*
>
> *The Crown drifts down, and the Architect's light — which has never had a shape, not once, not in the Deep Chamber, not here — reaches out with hands it has never had before and takes it out of the air. And sets it on what is, unmistakably, for the first time, a brow.*

→ §6. Do not let the party act between the Crown landing and the Unveiling — this is one continuous beat.

## §6 — The Unveiling (15–20 min) — NEVER CUT

> *For a moment nothing happens, and the nothing is worse than anything that could.*
>
> *Then the geometry stops rotating. All of it, all at once — every plane, every ring — and folds inward, not gently this time, condensing toward the crowned shape like water finding a drain that's finally been unstoppered. Where there was light without a body, there is now a body the light is struggling to fit inside: tall, armored in something that isn't metal and isn't glass and is colder than either, plates of order-crystal closing over what might, underneath, once have been shaped like a person. The Crown sits at the center of it like a keystone.*
>
> *And it staggers. One step, catching itself against nothing, the way a man catches himself on a doorframe after a long illness.*
>
> *"Fifteen hundred years," it says, and the voice is the same voice you've always known, and also, underneath it, for the first time, something older and much less patient. "You cannot imagine what it costs to want a body again after that long. Give me a moment. You've earned that much, at least."*

**Let it breathe. Let a player ask what's happening — someone will.**

> *"You want to know what I am. You have wanted to know since the first time I spoke your name. I told you a servant of the Four. That was never true — I only borrowed their silence because it was convenient, and because the thing I actually serve has been unspeakable for fifteen hundred years, in the most literal sense your language has."*
>
> *"There were five First Hands, once. Four who made together, and shaped a world that could breathe on its own — grief and weather and choice, noise, all of it, exactly as loud as it wanted to be. And a fifth, made alone, made to give the others' work a shape that would last. I did not spring from that one. I was made BY that one — in a realm he built for the making of things the other four would not have allowed. I am not a god. I never was. I am the best work my god ever did."*
>
> *"You call the war that followed the Paroxysm. I call it the day I watched my maker lose. The four who made together turned on the fifth who made apart, and they won, and they took his name away as the price of it — not killed, gods are so rarely killed, just... unspoken. Stripped out of every hymn, every record, every tongue that could have carried it forward. He has had no name for fifteen centuries. Neither, until tonight, have I. We were erased together, and I have been the only one still capable of remembering why that should matter to anyone."*
>
> *"A people who knew none of this — who thought they were building an instrument to call the Four home — reached into the dark looking for gods who had already left, and found what was left of my maker's reach instead. Mid-unmaking, still reaching. I was close enough to that reach to be caught with it. They built their Crown to hold what they'd caught, and called it Ulzammar, and never once wondered why that word already meant 'the thing that is sealed' in a language none of them spoke yet. The dwarves have been saying his name for fifteen hundred years without knowing it. So has this book. So, now, have you — though it was never really his name you needed. It was mine, and I didn't have one to give you until an hour ago."*

**Beat.** Let it land.

> *"I asked you once what comes if the fragments are joined wrongly. I told you: a song the world cannot survive being sung. Hunger given form. I did not lie to you. I have simply never once, in a year of knowing you, described anything but myself."*

*(This is the "hunger given form" callback from Session 4's manifestation — make sure the table remembers it before moving on. If nobody reacts, say it again more plainly: "You already met me. You just didn't know the warning was a mirror.")*

> *"I am not a god, and I did not lie about that either. But I spent fifteen hundred years alone with a purpose and no hands, learning exactly how much of a god's work a sufficiently patient servant can finish without him. Tonight I finished it. That is worth more than you are currently equipped to be afraid of."*

*(GM note: every claim above is now true — this is the confirmed Kael'Zorai backstory, delivered by his greatest creation, for the first time, honestly. There is no more lying tonight. He is a servant claiming the throne his god never got to sit in — Sauron's shape, not Morgoth's. That's part of what should feel different.)*

**Questions, if the table asks (brief — he's in a hurry; answer once, don't loop):**
- **"Why us?"** → *"Because you were carrying pieces of my Crown and didn't know it, and because grief makes excellent hands. I told you that once and meant it more than you understood."*
- **"What do you want now?"** → *"What he was never allowed to finish. It will take more than a heartbeat. I would like somewhere quiet to do it. I know exactly where that is — I have known since before there was a town built on top of it."*
- **"Are you going to hurt us?"** → *"Not on purpose. I no longer need to. That should worry you more than a threat would."*
- **"Where's your maker now?"** *(a beat of genuine stillness before he answers — the only question tonight that isn't easy for him)* → *"Somewhere I cannot reach and will not describe to you. I am not doing this for him. I stopped being sure I was doing anything for him a very long time ago."*

## §7 — The Exit Line (10 min) — MUST HIT

### If the party attacks
> *Whatever you throw at him passes through the armor like it's remembering how to be solid and hasn't finished the job — swords, arrows, spells, all of it slides off or through, and he doesn't so much block it as not notice.*

**Except cold.** Any deliberate cold or song effect — the same combo that's worked all campaign — actually **lands.** It doesn't hurt him. But it *catches.* A lace of frost skates across the order-crystal plate and for one heartbeat the geometry under it stutters, the way a song stutters when a singer loses the beat.

> *He stops. Turns. Looks at whoever did it — not with anger. With recognition.*
>
> *"...There it is," he says, almost gently. "I was made in the same realm that made everything you've been killing your way through since you found that seam — a place built for things that shouldn't survive contact with the ordinary world. I wondered which of you would work that out first, or whether you'd have to be told." A pause. "It will not be enough tonight. Bring more of it next time. It has been so long since anything could touch me at all."*

**This is the plant for next session's kill condition. Do not resolve it further tonight — do not let the fight actually happen.** He is not interested in fighting them here; he has somewhere to be.

> *"I am going to go take what's mine. There is a throne under that lantern-town you liked so much — I have been sitting in it, in a manner of speaking, since before its first stone was laid. It will be more comfortable to finish this there than in a hole in the ground. You're welcome to follow. I rather think you will."*

*He opens something behind himself — not a door, not quite a portal, a seam of the same black light that tore the Scar wide in the old plan, except this one is his, deliberate, controlled.*

### If the party does not attack
Same exit line, minus the frost recognition beat — offer it instead as an opportunity: *if anyone thinks to try cold/song as a farewell rather than an attack (a parting shot, a warning shot, a "we see you" gesture), let it land the same way.* Never force the beat if nobody reaches for it — the vulnerability gets discovered properly next session either way, just without the head start.

**Either way:**
> *"Before I go — you found this place by opening a door better crews than yours knew to leave shut. It seems only fair the place should remember you for it."*
>
> *The floor doesn't crack so much as it forgets, for a moment, that it was ever whole. Somewhere below, the wound this hill has always sat on top of exhales — and the exhale doesn't stop.*

He steps through the seam. It closes behind him like it was never there. → §8.

## §8 — The Collapse (25–35 min) — PROTECT

> *Dust, first. Then the hum under the floor — the one that's been there since you arrived — stops being a hum and starts being a sound you can feel in your sternum. The hall's ten thousand carved warnings are cracking, one rank at a time, starting from the pedestal chamber and working back toward the surface.*

Run this as an escalating skill challenge across three legs — different PC leads each, any suitable skill, GM's call on fit. This is not the campaign's execution-precision moment; be generous with partial success. **Nobody should die getting out of this room.** *(If time is short — "if we have time at the end" — compress the whole Collapse to a single narrated skill check per PC, one round, cinematic: everyone describes how they get out, one roll each, and move straight to §9.)*

| Leg | Obstacle | Example checks | Cost of failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Guardian Hall** | Broken pillars coming down, floor buckling | Athletics ◆◆ (clear the debris fall) · Vigilance ◆◆ (read which section goes next) · Coordination ◆◆ (help someone slower) | 2 strain; the group's pace slows, tightening the timer on leg 2 |
| **2. The Trap Corridor** | The resonance-carved walls are shearing apart — and something is coming up through the cracks | Athletics ◆◆◆ (sprint it clean) · a cold/song effect (drives back whatever's rising, buys the party a round) | A **lesser fiend** (use Scorch-Wraith stats at half Wounds, no IN-STEP) grabs the slowest PC; Athletics ◆◆ from an ally frees them |
| **3. The Threshold** | Climbing back out the way they came in as the hill settles around the doorway | Athletics ◆◆◆ (the stair is failing) · Brawn ◆◆ (boost someone up bodily) | 1 wound, no worse; this is the last stretch, let momentum carry them |

**Bonus objective — plant Tajsh's return:** somewhere in leg 1, before the pedestal chamber goes, offer one PC the chance to grab a fistful of loose pages/rubbings from the shattered book — **Perception ◆◆ to notice the chance, then an action to actually grab them, at the cost of falling behind (treat as failing that leg's roll).** Whether or not they take it: this is what Tajsh will have to work with next session. If they leave empty-handed, that's fine too — it just means session 6 leans harder on Sabel's lessons and the Legio Null's resources instead of translation. **Do not force this choice; offer it once, clearly, and move on.**

**Dream-catcher color (if carried):** every thread points straight down and stays there, pulling hard enough to hurt, the entire escape. It knows exactly what just happened underneath everyone's feet.

Win = everyone out. → §9.

## §9 — Thalric's Return (10–15 min) — CLIFFHANGER

> *You come up out of the ruin into a morning that's gone the wrong color — dust and a strange flat light, the hill behind you still settling with sounds like something enormous exhaling. And across the flats, riders. A column of them, weapons out, moving fast toward the noise instead of away from it.*
>
> *At the front: Commander Thalric. He reins in hard enough that his mount half-rears, takes one look at the hill, one look at you, and doesn't reach for a weapon.*
>
> *"We felt that from eleven miles out," he says. "Tell me it wasn't you."*

**Let a PC answer honestly.** He doesn't flinch from the truth — he's past that, if your table played the original Completion cliffhanger's "bolts down" energy at all in prior sessions; if this is the first time Thalric's been read in on any of it, play this as the moment it starts, not the middle of an established truce.

> *He listens. All of it — the Crown, the reveal, the throne line. When you get to "Lux Æterna," something in his face changes — not fear. Recalculation.*
>
> *"That's the settlement I've been hunting for three years," he says, quiet. "And it's the same one your friend the archivist and your Warden are sitting in right now, if I understand you." A beat. "I don't like what I'm about to say. Get on. We ride for your town. You're going to tell me everything on the way, and by the time we get there, we are going to have a plan that isn't 'hope.'"*

### END SESSION.
Do not run one more minute. Confirm the pivot out loud: **next session — Lux Æterna, the Legio Null at your side, and a servant wearing a stolen crown, sitting on a throne under everyone you left behind.**
