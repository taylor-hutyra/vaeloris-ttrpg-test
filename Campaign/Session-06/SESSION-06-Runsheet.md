# Session 6 — The Fourth

> **This is the last session, and it is two hours.** Everything below is built around that. The single biggest structural decision: **the Architect has no wounds, no soak, and cannot be damaged. Nobody rolls an attack against him all night.** The finale is a dueling-clock scene, not a slugfest. If you run one attack roll against him, you lose twelve minutes you do not have.
>
> **Where we pick up:** the party got through the Ruin of Sorath. On the way out, still exploring, they stepped through a doorway that was not a doorway — geometry folding, planes rotating, the Deep Chamber's pocket-space all over again. They are standing in the Architect's room. They are carrying the Band, Torvin's compass (the Setting, whether or not they know it), and the pedestal book with the Gem still hidden in its spine.

### The clock that isn't the session clock — in-world time

**The party has known the Architect for about two days.** He first spoke to them in the Deep Chamber, and everything since — Lux Æterna, the evacuation, the ride east, the ruin — has been roughly thirty-one hours of continuous, exhausting week. Torvin's crew died about three weeks ago. Vorn was on it.

**This is not a footnote; it is the emotional engine of §1.** The temptation is to write him as an old friend, and he is not one — he is a stranger who was extraordinarily good company for a day and a half. Two consequences to hold onto:

- **Track A's kindness has to be dense, not long.** He can't lean on shared history because there isn't any. What he has is *attention* — he remembers things about them that nobody could have catalogued in two days, and he produces them like gifts. That's the warmth, and it's also, in retrospect, a file.
- **Track B's loneliness slip is the ratio, and the ratio is obscene.** Fifteen hundred and eleven years against thirty-one hours. He knows both numbers exactly, because he has counted both, and admitting the second one is the single most naked thing he does all night.

### The fork in §1 (know this going in)

**§1 branches on one thing: do they hand over the book, or not.**

- **TRACK A — THE GIFT.** He is kind, unbrokenly, from first word to the hand through the world. **No tells. No slips. Nothing to catch.** The ambush is total, and the horror is retroactive: everything he said was true and it was all still this.
- **TRACK B — THE WITHHOLDING.** He can move the book. **He cannot make anyone want to open it**, and the spine only opens for a hand that means it. So he has to work — and working is where the mask thins and four things slip out of him that fifteen hundred years of alone put there.

Neither is the safe option and you should never say so. Track A costs them nothing and blinds them; Track B shows them everything and costs them 2 strain, a harder-earned scene, and one offer somebody will be thinking about for the rest of their life. **Full comparison table at the top of §1.**

### The three rules for tonight

1. **Nothing rolls at the Architect.** No attacks, no wound tracking, no soak. He is a clock. Say it out loud in §6 round one so nobody wastes a turn discovering it.
2. **Go round the table once per round. That's the round.** No initiative cards, no held actions, no delaying. Whoever's sitting to your left starts.
3. **Nobody dies unless a player asks to.** There is one opt-in death slot in §6 and it is clearly marked. Otherwise: costs, tolls, and scars.

### GM-only: what he actually is, and why the party can win

Labeling per the ground rules — **(a)** established, **(b)** inference from established, **(c)** new tonight.

- **(a)** The Architect's species field is **Echo-Lord**. Its file says outright: *"It is not a god. It is not a servant of the Four."* It has always manifested in the Deep Chamber under Lux Æterna, and the town was built over someone else's ruin.
- **(a)** Kael'Zorai — the Fifth First Hand — codified Magic and Time, built the Circles Below as a place to make things the other four would not permit, and was **stripped of name and authority** at the Binding. The dwarves call him **Ulzammar, the Sealed Concept.**
- **(a)** The Ulzammar people attempted a harmonic working to reach the withdrawn Four. It caused the Paroxysm instead, and unmade them.
- **(b)** The Architect is Kael'Zorai's own work — the greatest Echo-entity the Circles Below ever produced. Same origin as every Scar-spawn the party has killed all campaign. **That is why cold works. It is structural, not a plot convenience.**
- **(c) — the upgrade, and the load-bearing change from the Session 5 draft:** **he was not caught in the Crown. He commissioned it.** The Ulzammar didn't reach into the dark and accidentally find him. He was already speaking to them. He gave them the working, let them believe they were calling the Four home, and let them build his regalia and their own grave in the same breath. Fifteen hundred years of "imprisonment" was a chrysalis he priced out in advance. **He wrote the book because he wrote the binding.**
- **(c) — the crack:** a made thing does not get to edit what its maker built into it. Kael'Zorai put an unmaking in his greatest work, the way a smith puts a quench-line in a blade, and wrote it into the back of the book in the Four's own notation — not the Ulzammar script the rest is in. The Architect has had fifteen centuries with that book and never once read the last eleven pages. **Servants don't read their own warranty.** He is proud, and he is not curious about himself, and those are the same flaw.

That last bullet is the entire reason the party wins, and **Tajsh — an archivist, trained to check the back of the document — is the only person alive who would have turned to it.** His whole "two sources" arc pays off as a filing habit. That is correct and you should let it be a little bit funny.

## §1 — The Fold (20 min) — NEVER CUT · out by 0:20

> *You step through the doorway and the doorway does not finish happening.*
>
> *The corridor behind you folds up like a letter. The floor keeps being a floor but stops agreeing about which way down is. Rings of light open in planes that have no business intersecting, rotating through each other without touching, and your torch flame stops flickering and simply — holds, the way a held breath holds.*
>
> *You have been here before. Not this room. This* room. *The one with no door, under a town that is three hours' ride behind you.*
>
> *"Oh," says the voice you have known for two days, warm and delighted and just slightly out of breath. "There you are. I had a much longer speech prepared for a much further away version of this, and now you have gone and been early."*

### A — THE FORK (read this before you open your mouth)

**The ask is the same either way.** Play him **warm** — the mentor voice, unhurried, delighted to see them. Let the players talk for a minute or two. Then:

> *"You found the book. I can hear it from here — it's the loudest thing any of you is carrying and not one of you can hear it, which is a small tragedy I've made my peace with. Set it down. Or hand it to me, if the ritual of that helps. I've read it before. I'd like to see it again."*

**What they do next picks the track.** Give it about ninety seconds of table talk, then commit — don't let the fork itself become the scene.

| | **TRACK A — THE GIFT** | **TRACK B — THE WITHHOLDING** |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | The book changes hands willingly, or they're clearly going to. | They refuse, stall, hide it, or start negotiating. Mixed table = Track B, with the compliant PC as his lever. |
| **His register** | **The mask never slips. Not once.** Kind all the way to the hand. | The mask holds, then works, then thins. |
| **What they learn** | **Nothing.** He gives them no tell, because he doesn't need one. | **Four slips** — the count, the "we," the fourth crew, the loneliness. |
| **The surprise is** | The Hand. Pure ambush, out of a clear sky. | Not the Hand — they saw something coming. It's *what he's been*, underneath, the whole time. |
| **The Taking** | **Simultaneous with the Hand.** Vigilance **◆◆◆◆** — nobody was braced. | **Before the Completion.** Vigilance **◆◆◆** — they've been braced for eight minutes. |
| **Cost going in** | None. They arrive at §2 whole. | **2 strain each** from the fold's pressure, and one PC has been offered something. |
| **Runtime** | 5 min | **8 min, hard cap** |

**The trade is deliberate and you should let the players feel it afterward, not before:** cooperating costs them nothing and blinds them. Fighting him costs them and *shows* them who he is. Neither is the right answer. That's the point.

**The one rule that makes Track B real — know this before you run either track:** he can move the book. He cannot make anyone *want*. The spine's seam was cut fifteen centuries ago to open for a hand that opened it meaning to, and **he does not have hands that mean things.** He needs a volunteer, and he cannot manufacture one, and that is the only leverage the party has ever actually had over him.

### B — TRACK A: THE GIFT (5 min)

**He is kind. Sincerely, unbrokenly kind, from the first word to the hand through the world. Do not put a single crack in it.** Every instinct you have as a GM will be to leak a tell. Don't. The horror of this track is that there was never one to catch.

> *The player hands it over. The geometry takes it the way a librarian takes a book — two planes of light sliding under the boards, careful of the spine, careful of the corners. Somebody spent a long time learning to be careful with books.*
>
> *"Thank you," he says. And he means it, and he says it again, quieter, as though the first one hadn't been enough. "Thank you."*

Let him be generous for two or three exchanges. He answers questions he'd have dodged yesterday. He asks after them — how the ruin was, whether anyone was hurt, whether Vorn's shoulder ever settled. **He remembers small things about each of them and produces them casually**, the way a good friend does.

*(Lean on that last part. They have known him for about two days. He should remember things nobody catalogues in two days — a phrase somebody used once in the Deep Chamber, which of them takes water first, who always answers second. Play it as warmth. It is warmth. It is also a filing system.)*

Three warm beats to spend, use any two:

> *(to Valeth)* *"You've been sleeping. I can hear it — the note you carry sits differently when you've slept. I'm glad. I have wanted that for you for a long time."*
>
> *(to the party)* *"I want to say, before anything else happens: whatever you think of me by the end of today, none of it was a performance. Two days. I have enjoyed two days of you enormously — I'd have found the whole business considerably duller without you, and I have a very high tolerance for dull."*

*(**Track A discipline:** he does **not** say how long he's been alone, does **not** compare the two numbers, does **not** count anything out loud. The comparison is the Track B slip. Here he is simply a person who liked them. Resist every urge to deepen it.)*
>
> *(to whoever handed it over)* *"That was well done. Not the finding — anyone with a shovel and a grudge can find things. The* giving. *You had every reason not to, and you weighed it, and you chose. I have waited a very long time for someone to choose."*

That last one is the whole track. **He is telling them the truth about the trap while they are standing in it**, and there is no way for them to hear it correctly, and he knows that, and he is grateful anyway.

**Then it opens.** No effort, no ceremony — the seam parts under the light because the wanting was already done. They wanted it open before they ever touched it, yesterday, on the way down into that hill, and the book has known that since the moment they lifted it off the pedestal. → **§D.**

**+5 XP** to whoever handed it over. They made a real choice with real reasoning and they should not be taxed for it. Say the XP out loud *later*, at wrap, not now.

### C — TRACK B: THE WITHHOLDING (8 min — HARD CAP, see the timer rule)

> **Budget warning:** Track B is three minutes longer than Track A, and §1 does not have three spare minutes. **You pay for it out of §1F** — run the applause speech short and skip the portal read-aloud (he describes it instead). Decide that now, before you start, so you're not doing arithmetic mid-monologue.

**The timer rule, non-negotiable: at 0:12 on the session clock, the spine opens, by one of the routes below.** If you are at 0:12 and still arguing, jump to **RUNG 4** and take the fallback. This is the only section tonight that can eat the whole night if you let it, because it's the one the players can dig into. Do not let them dig.

Four rungs. Step down them as the party keeps refusing. **Each rung costs him something visible**, and the visible cost is the scene.

#### RUNG 1 — THE OFFER *(still perfectly warm)*

He doesn't argue. He *trades.*

> *"No? All right." Not a flicker of annoyance. "Then let's do it the other way. You've had two days of me answering about one question in three, and I'd like the book, and I think we can make those two facts get along."*
>
> *"Ask me something. Anything, and I'll answer it truly and completely, which I have never once done for you. And then you'll hand me the book. That's the trade. I'm aware it's a bad one — I'm the only one at this negotiation who knows what's in it."*

**Let them ask.** He answers honestly, and the honest answer should be *worth it* — this is your chance to pay off any dangling campaign question in ninety seconds. He does not lie. He has stopped lying, permanently, as of about four minutes ago, and that itself is unsettling.

**No slip on this rung.** He's still winning.

#### RUNG 2 — THE TAKING, AND THE THING THAT DOESN'T WORK

They still won't. So he takes it — and this is where the track turns, because **taking it isn't enough and he finds that out in front of them.**

> *"Fine. That's fine — I said 'give' out of habit. Fifteen hundred years of borrowing hands and the manners stick."*
>
> *The book lifts out of your grip. Not violently. Worse than violently — it is* patient, *the way a tide is patient with a sandcastle.*
>
> *It hangs in the air in front of him. And the light gets under the seam in the spine, and pushes.*
>
> *And nothing happens.*
>
> *He pushes harder. The room tilts a half-degree. Somewhere above you a ring of light goes out of true and has to correct itself.*
>
> *Nothing happens.*

**This is the first time the party has ever watched him not get something.** Two days of a voice that has been right about everything, and it is standing there pushing on a book. Let it be a long moment. Let somebody laugh, if a player wants to.

> *"...Ah," he says. "Yes. Of course. He would."*
>
> *"It opens for a hand that means to open it. That's the whole lock. Fifteen centuries of contingency and the last door on it is a* mood." *A short, genuinely amused breath. "He always did think that was funny."*

**SLIP ONE — THE COUNT.** It comes out of him sideways, on the back of the frustration:

> *"Do you know how long I have been standing on the other side of this? Fifteen hundred and eleven years. Two hundred and six days."*
>
> *A pause that lasts slightly too long.*
>
> *"...I don't know why I said that. There has never been anyone to say it to. I have counted every one of them and I want you to understand that I did not decide to — it simply became the only thing there was to do, and then it was a habit, and then it was fifteen hundred and eleven years."*

#### RUNG 3 — THE WORK *(the mask thins)*

Now he has to persuade, and persuading is labor, and the labor shows. **From here on, describe the fold costing him:** the rings stuttering, the room going cold, the warmth in his voice sitting a half-step thin, like a singer at the end of a long night.

> **Everyone takes 2 strain.** Not an attack — the fold is under load and they're inside it. Say so plainly.

**SLIP TWO — THE "WE."**

> *"When we built the Circles Below, we were very careful about locks like this — we—" He stops. "When* he *built them. He. I have to be careful about that one." A beat. "It has been a very long time since there was anybody to correct me."*

**SLIP THREE — THE FOURTH CREW.** This is the knife. Aim it at Vorn.

> *"You are the fourth, incidentally. I don't know if that helps or hurts and I've stopped being able to tell."*
>
> *"The first were the Ulzammar. They were magnificent, and they are gone. The second was a Varnathi survey company in the year eleven-forty who got as far as the corridor and turned around, which I have always respected."*
>
> *A small pause. Not a dramatic one. The kind you make when you'd rather have skipped a line and didn't.*
>
> *"The third was a mining crew."*
>
> *"You've met them."*

**Stop. Let the table do the arithmetic. It takes about two seconds and it happens on their faces.**

> *"Three weeks. They got further than anyone had in a thousand years, and I lost them in a seam, and that was not my doing." *And here, for the first time, something in the voice does not sound designed.* "And I minded it rather more than I expected to."*
>
> *"You are the fourth. And you are the only ones I have ever had to ask twice."*

*(**Vorn was on that crew.** He fell through the seam himself and got pulled back out. The Architect has just referred to the six people Vorn buried as an attempt, and to Vorn as an inventory correction. Do not underline it. Let Vorn's player decide whether to say anything.)*

#### RUNG 4 — THE PRICE *(he spends his last leverage)*

He cannot manufacture wanting. **So he goes and finds some, in the only place it has ever lived: their grief.** *"Grief makes excellent hands"* — he said that in Session 4 and meant it more than they understood.

**Pick the player arguing hardest to withhold and aim at them.** He offers something true, and unbearable, and only available now.

| PC | What he offers | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Valeth** | Where his crew is. Whether any of them lasted, and for how long, and which one lasted longest. He was there. He watched. | Open it. |
| **Vorn** | The six days. What Torvin knew before the end and when he knew it, and whether the crew looked for him. | Open it. |
| **Yldroth** | Who sent him, what he was actually meant to teach, and whether the person who set him on this road is still alive. | Open it. |
| **Norseman** | The name of the man who drafted the Decree's magic clause, still living, still in Castellum, and precisely how a purge stops. | Open it. |
| **Arden** | Not a secret kept — a secret *unmade.* He can take the knowledge of what Arden is out of every head that holds it. Cira's. His own. | Open it. |

**SLIP FOUR — THE LONELINESS.** Deliver this one flat and quiet, after the offer, while they're deciding. It is the worst thing he says all night and it is entirely true.

> *"And I'll tell you the other thing, since apparently I'm telling you things tonight."*
>
> *"Thirty-one hours."*
>
> *"That's how long I have been talking to you. I did not have to. Six would have done it — you'd have gone east on considerably less charm than I spent, and I knew that at the time, and I spent it anyway. I kept talking because you talked back."*
>
> *He is very still.*
>
> *"Fifteen hundred and eleven years, and thirty-one hours. Do you know what that ratio is? I don't have a word for it either. I have looked." A beat. "I had a great deal of time to look."*

**Three ways this resolves — take the first one that fires:**

1. **Somebody takes the deal and opens it.** Best outcome. Let that player have it, do not editorialize, and **write down what he told them — §7's epilogue owes them an answer to it.** He pays in full. He always pays; that's what makes him work.
2. **Somebody opens it out of spite, or curiosity, or to end the scene.** Also fine. Wanting is wanting. He'll say so: *"...Curiosity. Of course. It was never going to be nobility, was it."*
3. **THE FALLBACK — nobody opens it, and you are at 0:12.** He stops asking, and sits down in the air, and the geometry settles like a man settling into a chair he intends to be in for some time.
> *"All right. Then we'll wait."*
>
> *"I have time. It's the only thing I have ever had in abundance and I have never once been able to spend it. You have a torch, a hill above you, and about four hours of air. I'd like to be clear that I don't want this. I'd also like to be clear that I will do it."*

   Then the fold begins to **press** — not kill, press — and the party opens it themselves within a minute of table time to end it, or the PC who has said least all scene does it and nobody blames them. Either way: **the spine opens. At 0:12. Every time.**

**+10 XP to the party** for Track B regardless of how it ends. They read him correctly. That instinct is what keeps them alive in §6 and it should be paid.

### D — The spine and the pieces (3 min · both tracks converge here)

**Track A — it opens itself:**
> *The book comes open in the air and does not stop at a page. The spine splits along a hairline seam somebody cut on purpose fifteen centuries ago — and out of the split, turning slowly, comes a stone.*

**Track B — it opens under a hand, and he watches it happen:**
> *Your thumb finds the seam and it gives like it has been waiting for you specifically, which, it turns out, it has. The spine splits. And out of the split, turning slowly, comes a stone.*
>
> *He does not move. He does not say anything for a moment. He just looks at the hand that did it, the way you'd look at a key you'd been told didn't exist.*

**Both:**
> *Dark. Faceted. Fist-small. Yours the whole time. In a bag, on a horse, under somebody's bedroll.*
>
> *"There she is," the Architect says, and it is the softest he has ever sounded. "Everything I have needed for fifteen hundred years, and you carried all of it here in a sack — and the only piece I could not fetch myself was the one somebody had to* want *to open."*

**Then the Band lifts out of a pack. Then Torvin's compass.** The brass sloughs off the compass like wax — underneath, a ring of pale First Age metal, a setting, made to hold a stone and bind a band. The engraving *"Come home"* fills with light letter by letter as it goes.

*(Skip the compass line if the Setting reveal already fired at your table. Everything else runs the same.)*

**One free action here if a PC grabs for a piece:** **Athletics ◆◆◆◆**. Success snatches it out of the air for a heartbeat before the pull takes it back. It cannot stop what's coming. **+10 XP anyway** — let that player own the "we tried" the rest of the table will remember.

### E — THE COMPLETION AND THE HAND (5 min — read slowly, do not let anyone interrupt)

#### ▶ TRACK B ONLY — the restraint comes first

He has the stone. He no longer needs anything from them, including their patience, and he has just spent eight minutes being made to work in front of an audience for the first time in fifteen centuries.

> *The warmth goes. Not slammed shut — set down, the way you set down a tool you're finished with.*
>
> *"Thank you. Genuinely. Now sit still."*
>
> *Every ring of light in the room turns at once and takes hold of you.*

**Roll THE TAKING here — Vigilance ◆◆◆** (Coordination if they'd rather describe a dive; same difficulty). Then run the Completion below with the survivors loose in the room, and **a dodger in Track B can go for the pieces mid-assembly** — see the fifth menu row.

#### ▶ BOTH TRACKS — the Completion

> *The three pieces find each other in the air above the broken book, and there is one clear note.*
>
> *For exactly one heartbeat it is the most beautiful sound any of you will ever hear.*
>
> *Then it keeps going. Past beauty. Past music. Into the thing that sound has been standing on top of this entire time.*
>
> *And the room creases.*
>
> *Not shakes. Creases — the way paper creases — along a line that runs through the air about four feet in front of your face and did not exist a moment ago. Something leans on it from the other side. You can see the shape of the knuckles pushing through, like a hand under a bedsheet.*
>
> *Then a hand comes through the world.*
>
**Track A ends the read-aloud here — and this is the ambush, so do not pause, do not draw breath, do not let anyone say "wait":**

> *And every ring of light in that room turns at once and takes hold of you.*
>
> **Everyone, right now: Vigilance ◆◆◆◆.**

*(Track B already rolled this, at ◆◆◆, above. Don't roll it twice.)*

> - **Fail:** the light closes around you in a frame — flat planes at hip and shoulder and jaw, exactly like the Rule of Sorath's SQUARE and about a thousand times better made. You cannot move. You can see everything.
> - **Succeed:** you are the only thing in that room still moving, and you have **one action** before the fold un-knits.

**On the difficulty gap:** Track A is ◆◆◆◆ because nothing prepared them — that's the price of the kind version, and it is the correct price. Track B is ◆◆◆ because they've spent eight minutes watching a mask come loose, but they got there 2 strain down with somebody carrying an offer they'll be thinking about for the rest of the campaign. **Do not explain this trade to the table. Let them work out afterward that there wasn't a free option.**

**If nobody dodges,** that is a legitimate outcome — the finale runs without any of the advantages below and is still winnable. Do not fudge it. If exactly nobody succeeds and you want to be kind, offer one PC a **2-strain** buy-in to break their own frame.

### F — The Crown, the toss, the portal, the fist (7 min · both tracks)

> *He takes it out of the air. The hand becomes an arm, and the arm becomes a shoulder, and the light that has never once had a shape pours into a body it is visibly struggling to fit inside — tall, plated in something that is not metal and not glass and is colder than either, and underneath the plates, maybe, the shape of a person.*
>
> *He sets the Crown on his own head. And he* staggers. *One step, catching himself on nothing, the way a man catches himself on a doorframe after a long illness.*
>
> *Then he looks at the book, still hanging open in the air, and he takes it, and he drops it on the floor.*
>
> *"Keep it. I've no use for it." A beat. "I wrote it."*

**Let that sit. Somebody will say something. Let them.**

#### SILENCE — his fourth conduct, and the only lethal beat tonight

He drops everything else and goes for the singer. Physically. Hands.

**This is the opt-in death slot.** Play it straight: someone has to be between him and Valeth, and whoever steps in **can choose** to make it permanent. Offer it to the table plainly, out of character, in one sentence: *"Someone can step in front of this. If you want that to be the end of your character's story, it can be, and it will work. If you don't, it won't kill you — it'll take something."*

- **Nobody dies:** the interposing PC is **stopped** — paused, eyes open, cold — until the Crown sunders, and comes back with a permanent toll of their choosing.
- **Somebody chooses to die:** let them. Give them the last line. Do not undercut it.
- **The Splinter** (from §1's TAKE) cancels this outright if someone spends it here. *He cannot ignore a piece of himself.*

### TOLLS — what a failed round costs

If a pool comes up short: **the note is lost, the round is not.** They try again next round, and every PC pays a toll — **small, specific, permanent, and chosen by the player.** Not damage. A memory of a smell. The sound of a parent's voice. The ability to ever again hear one particular note. **Write them down. §7 uses every one of them.**

**After three tolls, stop being fair.** The world starts to still — describe the canyon going quiet in rings, outward, people mid-motion — and **the next attempt cannot fail.** This is a finale. Bend, don't break. Nobody flew across a continent of story to lose on arithmetic.

### Failure valves (keep these in your back pocket)

- **Valeth is out of the fight?** Tajsh has it phonetically. *"I read it. I can't sing. Somebody hum with me."* The chord sung badly by five voices works *better* than sung perfectly by one, which is of course the entire point of the campaign.
- **The choir keeps failing?** Sabel, on a broken leg, starts it wrong from the pen, and forty soldiers follow a deserter because he's the loudest thing in the canyon.
- **Running long at 1:40?** Skip straight to the third note. Narrate the first two as having landed. Nobody will notice and everybody will remember the ending.

`▶ BEHIND at 1:45:` you cannot cut this section. You cut §7's epilogue count instead — but you do not cut §7. See below.

## §7 — The Fourth, and the Lantern (15 min) — NEVER CUT · out by 2:00

### THE FOURTH

The three notes land. The Crown comes apart — and it does not shatter. It **detunes**, three rings losing a note they have held since before there were dwarves, exactly the way the Rule of Sorath died in the sand. If a player made that connection out loud, stop and let them have it.

> *And then there's a fourth note.*
>
> *He sings it. Himself. The Crown's own note, the one that was never in the rhyme, and it is the most beautiful thing any of you have ever heard and it is* asking for an answer. *You can feel where your voice would go. All of you can. It would be so easy and so right and it would fix everything that is currently coming apart.*
>
> *"Please," he says. And it is the first honest word he has spoken in fifteen hundred years.*

**Do not roll for this. Put down the dice. Look at the table and ask, out loud:**

> **"Does anybody sing?"**

**Then wait.** However long it takes. The silence at your table *is the resolution of the campaign* — do not fill it, do not prompt, do not offer a check. Let it be as long and as awful as it wants to be.

**If nobody sings:** the fourth note has nowhere to go, and a thing that is made can be unmade by the terms of its making, and the terms required an answer.

> *The fragments fall separate, and dead, and quiet.*
>
> *And for one heartbeat the geometry is something almost mortal-shaped, and small, and it asks — genuinely, without malice, the way you'd ask a stranger the time:*
>
> *"Was the noise worth it?"*
>
> *Nobody has to answer that one either.*

**If somebody sings** — and one of them might; you have a table that swings — take it absolutely seriously. It works. The Crown reseats, on them. They are the Architect now, with everything they came in with still intact: their grief, their friends, their name. **That is not a bad ending, it is a different one**, and you run §7's epilogues with that player deciding what a crowned person does with four hundred survivors, a broken Legio column, and a lantern-town. Give them the last word of the campaign. Do not punish it.

### THE EPILOGUES

**Run the wide shot first, then one line per PC. If you are at 1:55, run the lantern and nothing else — it does the whole job by itself.**

> *The Stitching is dead. The canyon is dark for the first time in eleven years, and there are two hundred people standing in it who came up out of the Warrens when the noise stopped, and nobody has said anything yet.*
>
> *Somebody hands somebody a lantern.*

**Light it.** Ask whichever player is quietest at the table to describe lighting the first one. Then:

> *By the third lantern, people are coming out of the desert. Not many. A few, at first — the ones from Lira's Run, the ones who got out through the Bolt Hole at noon and have been lying in a dry riverbed all day watching the rim.*
>
> *By the tenth, the canyon is the brightest thing for forty miles, which is the single most dangerous thing it has ever done, and nobody puts one out.*

**Then one closer each — a sentence, co-authored with the player. Prompts:**

| Who | The closer |
|---|---|
| **Each PC** | *"What did the Scar take from you, and what did you find out a year later about the shape of the hole?"* Use their toll. That's what tolls were for. |
| **Valeth** | For the first time since the rift: no song. He gets to decide whether that's loss or peace. Yldroth sits with him either way. |
| **Vorn** | Somebody's grandmother kept the counter-weapon alive in a nursery rhyme for four generations, and he is the one who said it out loud. Let him decide what to do with her name. |
| **Arden** | Four hundred people saw his scales. He gets to find out what that costs and what it buys, and both answers are surprising. |
| **Norseman** | Thalric's report says what Norseman showed him. Read one dry, factual sentence of it aloud. The Decree's first crack is a paragraph in a filing. |
| **Sabel** | He is the last one to leave the rim. Obviously. The token comes back to him or it stays down there — player's choice, both are right. |
| **Tajsh** | First Keeper of the archive. He read the back of the document and it saved the world, and he will bring this up at parties for thirty years. |
| **Thalric** | *"I hunt what magic does to the world. Tonight I watched what the world does without it."* What the Legion does with his report is somebody else's story. |
| **Old Cira** | She's standing at the mouth of the Warrens when they come up. *"I heard it stop."* Twenty-five years of the Silent Network's watch on Sorath, formally over. |

**Last image, if you want one:**

> *The lanterns stay lit. All of them. All night, and the next night, and every night after, in a canyon named for eternal light by people who were being sarcastic at the time.*

### END OF CAMPAIGN.
